How Race Condition Leading to Business Logic Flaws Affecting Features

[Web Security] Mar 2, 2026 • 4 min read

Impact Focus: Group uniqueness validation & Subscription enforcement limits.

The First Bug: Group Duplication & Denial of Control

While browsing the website, I noticed a feature for creating groups and decided to test its logic. After creating a group, I tried creating another group with the exact same name.

The application blocked this action. Naturally, I decided to test for a Race Condition on the creation endpoint. Sending parallel requests succeeded! ✅

While each group received a unique ID, creating duplicate group names triggered an unexpected business logic error. I discovered that I couldn’t edit any group with duplicate names. Even as the owner, I couldn't reduce member privileges or manage settings—effectively losing administrative control over my own groups.

Second Bug: Bypassing Subscription Member Limits

Since one endpoint was vulnerable to race conditions, I tested others. I focused on the endpoint responsible for adding team members.

The free tier allowed a maximum of 5 members. Attempting to add a 6th member was properly rejected by the UI and API.

Figure 1: Application rejecting the 6th member under normal conditions

Using Burp Suite's Repeater (Send in parallel / Turbo Intruder), I performed a race condition attack while sending invitations to exceed the limit.

Figure 2: Sending parallel invitation requests via Burp Suite

It succeeded! ✅ I was able to invite more than 5 members, completely bypassing the subscription limit.

Figure 3: Successfully adding members beyond the allowed limit

Bug Bounty Insight: Impact vs. Exclusions

During reporting, I faced a common hurdle: Scope and Exclusions policies. The Subscription Bypass (Bug 2) was marked as an exclusion under "product feature usage limits". Bypassing numerical limits is often treated as a known limitation rather than a security risk to user data.

Figure 4: Bug Bounty policy regarding feature usage limits

Key Takeaway

  • Member Bug (Bug 2): Pure usage limit bypass → High chance of Informational / Out of Scope.
  • Group Duplication (Bug 1): If framed only as "duplicate names", it gets rejected. But when framed as Broken Access Control (loss of admin control over privileges), it moves from a "rate limit" issue to a critical logic error!
